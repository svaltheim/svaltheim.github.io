Hello!

After many years away from _reverse engineering_, I have decided to get back into it. I have always been interested in understanding how things work under the hood: how malware manages to bypass security measures and remain undetected, what actually happens when we run a game or an application, how an EXE works, what role DLLs play, and how programs interact with different parts of the operating system.

So, I have decided to rediscover this world and, at the same time, document my investigations and analysis on this blog. Not only as a way to keep a record of what I discover, but also as a way to push myself to explain what I have learned and make sure I really understand the concepts.

The idea is for this space to serve as a technical research notebook. Reverse engineering and systems architecture will certainly have their place, but they won't be the only subjects covered here. There will also be room for other areas of security that interest me, such as pentesting, bug bounty hunting, Red Team operations, and application security.

I might publish several articles about reverse engineering, only for the next one to be about something I came across while investigating an API, a tool that caught my attention, or a machine I solved on a platform. I don't want to restrict the content to a single topic or follow a predefined path. I'd rather let my own research, experiments, and curiosity shape the direction of the blog.

There won't be a fixed publishing schedule either. I'll write whenever I have something interesting to share and, above all, when I feel I've done enough research to explain it with a reasonable level of technical rigor.
